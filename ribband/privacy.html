Privacy Policy of the app Ribband

Last updated: 24 November 2026. This policy applies to the Android app Ribband (package com.tobkum.ribband), version 1.0 and later, and to Ribband’s browser extension (section 12), until a newer version of this policy replaces it. It is published at https://tobiaskummer.de/ribband/privacy.html.

1. In short

Ribband keeps the links and articles you save on your device. There is no account with me, and I run no server that receives your library. The app shows no ads, contains no analytics or tracking tools of its own, and does not sell or share your data. Some things do go over the internet, because that is how a page is fetched, how sync through your own Google Drive works and how a purchase through Google Play works. This policy names each of them. If you use Ribband’s browser extension, what you save with it goes only into Ribband’s hidden folder in your own Google Drive (section 12).

3. What stays on your device

Everything you save and do in the app is stored on your device (and, if you turn on sync, your library also in your own Google Drive; see section 4): the links you save, the text and pictures of articles, their titles (including titles you give them), tags, shelves, favourites, saved searches, highlights and notes, reading positions, your settings, Listen’s queue, and page reports you have not sent. I have no access to any of it.

Android’s backup to Google does not include this app. If you move to a new phone with Android’s own device-to-device transfer (Android 12 and later), your library comes along; sync settings, website sign-ins and purchase information do not.

4. What goes over the internet, and when

Fetching what you save. When you save a link, the app downloads that page from its website, as a browser would, and the pictures in it. When the app fetches the text of a link saved as http://, it tries https:// first and uses http:// only if that fails. The check whether a saved link still exists, the card for a link that is not an article, and pictures whose address begins with http:// are fetched as addressed; only the missing pictures the app fetches when you open an article are tried over https:// first. Pictures are always fetched by the device itself from the site that hosts them, never through me or through Google Drive: when you save, when sync brings an article to another device, and when you open an article whose pictures are missing and the device is online. Some pages are rendered in a hidden browser view so their text can be read; that view also loads what the page itself loads, such as scripts and services of other companies the website uses. The website, and those services, see your device’s IP address, a general browser identification and the list of your device’s languages, as they would in a browser. If a page was not available or could not be read, the app may fetch it again later on its own (once, about a day later). After an import, the app fetches the text of the imported links in the background. For links to videos, posts, music and similar pages, the app asks the same platform for the title and picture (its “oEmbed” service). When you open a saved link that is not an article, the app asks its website once whether the page is still there.

The app sends no cookies with these requests, except one case: if you open a website’s original page in the app and sign in or accept a consent there, that website keeps cookies in the app, and the app sends that same website’s cookies when it saves further pages from it. Settings › About your data › Clear website data removes them.

The original page. When you open the original page of a saved link, the app shows that website in a built-in browser view, with everything it loads, as a browser would. Third-party cookies are turned off there.

Android’s web component. The app’s web views are provided by Android System WebView, a part of Android made by Google. In the original-page view, Android’s Safe Browsing may ask Google whether an address you open is safe, to warn you about dangerous sites (Google’s protocol sends shortened hashes of addresses, not the addresses themselves, in its usual mode). The reader and the hidden view that renders pages when you save them have Safe Browsing turned off. The app also tells Android System WebView not to send Google its usage metrics. Safe Browsing is Google’s function under Google’s privacy policy; I receive nothing from it.

The Internet Archive. When you tap “Look for a copy on the Internet Archive”, the app sends the page’s address and the day you saved it to the Internet Archive (archive.org), and only then. If there is a copy, the app downloads it and its pictures from web.archive.org and keeps it on your device as that page’s text. With sync on, the copy travels with your library like any other text, and its pictures may load again from web.archive.org on your other devices or when you open it. The Internet Archive’s terms and privacy policy apply to these requests.

Sync through your own Google Drive (optional, off by default). If you turn on sync, Google asks you to choose a Google account and to allow Ribband a hidden folder in that account’s Google Drive (the “app data folder”, permission drive.appdata). Ribband can see only that folder, not your other files. The app then copies your library there: links, titles, tags and shelves, saved searches, highlights, notes, reading positions, what you archive or delete, the text of articles, and a small file per device with the device’s model name (for example “Pixel 8”) and a random-looking device number. Pictures and reader settings are not synced; each device downloads pictures itself (see above). On mobile data, a large backlog (more than about 50 MB in one sync run) waits for Wi-Fi unless you turn on “Catch up over mobile data”; what you save now is synced at once on any network. If you later connect a different Google account, sync pauses and asks you before it combines the two libraries. The connection is encrypted (HTTPS). To show which account is in use, the app asks Google for the account’s e-mail address and keeps it on the device; it is removed when you turn sync off. The data is stored by Google under your account and Google’s terms; I cannot see it. Sync needs Google Play services. While sync is set up, Settings › Sync › Turn off or remove… › Delete synced data from Google Drive deletes the copy and turns sync off. Once sync is off, you delete the copy in Google Drive on the web under Settings › Manage apps › Ribband › Options › Delete hidden app data. If your other devices still sync, turn sync off on them first; otherwise one of them copies its library there again. Turning sync off does not take back the access you gave Ribband to that folder; you remove it in your Google Account under Linked apps.

Buying Pro through Google Play. Pro is bought, paid for and restored through Google Play. Google processes the payment under its own terms and privacy policy; I never receive your payment details. Google Play reports each order to me with the order information it gives developers (the order number, the date, the product, the price with its tax, and the country of the billing address). Each time the app starts, and when you return to it after a few minutes away, it asks Google Play whether Pro has been bought with the Google account used in Google Play, so that a purchase made on Google Play itself reaches the app; it also asks when you open Pro, buy or restore it. Until Pro is yours, it also asks in the background, about once a day for up to two weeks after you last opened the app and twice a day while a payment is pending, so that Google Play gets the confirmation it needs within three days of a payment even if the app stays closed. Nothing about your library is sent with these questions. While the app uses it, Google Play’s billing component may send Google information about how it is working.

Page reports. “Report this page” stores a report on your device: the page’s address and title, how the app read the page, the language, the word count, your comment, the app version, the time of the report, a random number for the report and the saved link’s number in the app (random, with the time you saved the link in it), never the article’s text. Only if you tap Send by email does your own mail app open with the report, addressed to me, so you can read it before you send it. If you send it, I receive the e-mail with your e-mail address and use it only to improve how the app reads pages and, if needed, to reply. I delete reports within 12 months after receiving them.

Listen. Listen reads articles aloud with the text-to-speech engine on your device. The app does not send the text anywhere, and it does not choose a voice that needs the internet: where the engine marks a voice as needing a network, the app passes it over for one on the device. If the engine does not tell the app which voices it has, the engine chooses the voice itself. The speech engine is a separate app on your phone, with its own terms.

Nothing to me. Apart from page reports you send yourself, nothing from the app reaches me. The app contains no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking libraries and uses no advertising ID.

5. Permissions

  • Internet and network state: to fetch pages, sync and buy Pro, and to say “offline” when there is no connection.
  • Notifications (asked for once, when you first use Listen or start a long import or export, Android 13 and later): the playback controls while an article is read aloud, and the progress of an import or export. Both work without it.
  • Foreground service for media playback, wake lock: so Listen can keep reading with the screen off.
  • Foreground service for data transfer: so an import or export you started finishes when you leave the app. It reads and writes only the file you chose.
  • Run at start-up: used by Android’s job scheduler so background work (fetching saved pages, sync, the purchase check) continues after a restart.
  • Google Play billing: to buy and restore Pro.

Apart from these, and one internal permission that Android’s support library adds so the app’s own parts can signal each other, the app requests no permissions. It does not ask for your location, contacts, files, camera, microphone or accounts.

6. Legal bases (GDPR)

Most processing happens only on your device, under your control. Where I or the services above process personal data because of the app:

  • fetching pages, sync, purchases and Listen are done because you ask for them, to provide the app’s functions (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR);
  • the cookies and stored website data in the built-in browser view are stored because you open the page and sign in there (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG: strictly necessary for the service you asked for);
  • the order information Google Play gives me is kept because tax law requires it (Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR);
  • the app asks Google Play about Pro when it starts, when you return to it and, until Pro is yours, in the background for up to two weeks after you last opened it, also if you have never opened Pro, so that a purchase made on Google Play itself reaches the app and is confirmed in time (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; my legitimate interest is that a purchase made outside the app works in it);
  • some fetches happen without you asking at that moment: the second attempt about a day later, the texts of imported links, and what a saved page loads in the hidden view (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; my legitimate interest is that the links you save or import can be read in the app, which is what you save them for);
  • page reports you send are processed to fix how the app reads pages and to answer you (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, my legitimate interest in improving the app; Art. 6(1)(b) where you ask for a reply).

7. Who else receives data

I do not sell, rent or share your data. Data reaches the following only as described in section 4: the websites you save and the services they load; the platforms whose oEmbed service is asked; Google (Google Drive if you turn on sync, and for the browser extension, section 12; Google Play for purchases; Android System WebView’s Safe Browsing in the original-page view); the Internet Archive if you tap that action. Google may process data outside the European Union; see Google’s privacy policy for the safeguards it uses.

8. How long data is kept

  • On your device: until you delete it. Deleted items stay in Recently deleted for 30 days, then are removed. Uninstalling the app removes everything on the device.
  • In your Google Drive: until you delete it (see section 4). Within the folder, a short record that an item was deleted for good stays so that your other devices learn of the deletion. The text of an article that left Recently deleted after 30 days stays in the folder for another 90 days, in case you had restored it on another device; if you delete an article for good yourself, its text is removed at once. Replaced versions of an article’s text are removed after 30 days.
  • A page report you have not sent is removed with its article.
  • Page reports you sent me: deleted within 12 months after I receive them.
  • Pages saved with the browser extension, and what it keeps in the browser: see section 12.
  • Purchase records: kept by Google Play under its terms; the order information Google Play gives me is kept as long as tax law requires me to keep it.

9. Children

Ribband is not directed at children. I do not knowingly receive data from children; the app sends me nothing unless a page report is sent by e-mail. If you believe a child has sent me a report, contact me and I will delete it.

10. Security

Your library is stored in the app’s private storage on your device, protected by Android. Sync and purchases use encrypted connections. When the app fetches the text of a link that begins with http://, it tries the encrypted https:// first and fetches it without encryption only if that fails, as browsers with an HTTPS-first mode do. The other requests to addresses that begin with http:// (see section 4) are not encrypted.

11. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and to object to processing based on legitimate interests (Art. 21). Most of your data is never with me: you can see, export (Settings › Import and export › Export everything: one file with web pages, Markdown files and a copy the app can import again) and delete it in the app yourself. For page reports you sent me, or an order you want me to look up by its number, write to tobiaskummer@googlemail.com.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, in particular in the EU member state of your residence or work.

12. The browser extension

Ribband has a browser extension for Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera and Vivaldi, and one for Firefox. It does one thing: it saves the page in the current tab to Ribband on your phone. It works only if you use sync (section 4), because it goes through the same hidden folder in your own Google Drive. It is free, needs no account with me and uses no server of mine.

What it reads, and when. Only when you click its button or use its keyboard shortcut, and only in that tab: the page’s address, its title, and the page as the browser has loaded it at that moment, including parts that are not on screen. If you choose “Save link to Ribband” on a link, it reads that link’s address and text instead; your phone fetches the page behind it itself. It asks the browser for no access to all websites; the browser lets it read a tab only for that click.

What it removes first. Before the page leaves the browser, the extension removes scripts, hidden form fields, what is typed into forms, text you are writing in an editor on the page (a mail draft, for example), comments, pictures, and the values of the page’s data attributes except those Ribband needs to find the article. What the page contains as text stays in it, for example your name in a website’s menu, and so do its links, with everything their addresses contain. Your phone keeps only the article it finds in the page and deletes the page itself once the article is saved.

Where the page goes. Into the hidden app folder in your Google Drive that sync uses (permission drive.appdata): one small file with the page’s address exactly as it is (with anything the address contains), its title, the time of the save, the extension’s version number and the tags and shelf you chose for it, if any, and one with the page, compressed. The files are stored by Google under your account and Google’s terms, as for sync (section 4); I cannot see them, and nothing goes to me or to anyone else. Your phone takes the save at its next sync, saves the article as if you had shared the link, and then deletes both files. Pictures are not sent: your phone fetches them from the website as for any save, so a picture the website shows only to signed-in readers can be missing.

What else it reads in that folder. To know whether one of your phones takes saves from the browser, and when it last synced, it reads the small file each of your devices keeps in the folder (section 4). So that you can give a save tags and a shelf, it reads the list of the names of your tags and shelves that your phone keeps in the same folder. It reads nothing else of your library; only if Google Drive cannot find the device files by their names does it look through the names of the files in the folder to find them. To show which account is connected, it asks Google for the account’s e-mail address. In Chrome and other Chromium browsers it keeps that address in the browser and gives it only to Google, when it renews its access to your Google Drive, so that Google does not have to ask you again.

Signing in. You connect the extension in Google’s own sign-in window and allow it access only to that hidden folder, as for sync.

What stays in the browser. In the storage the browser gives extensions: the connected account’s e-mail address; the access Google gives the extension, which the browser forgets when it closes (in Firefox also a token with which the extension renews that access, so that you need not sign in each time); the names of tags and shelves you newly type there, until the browser closes; a short list of its own saves (an internal number, the time and a fingerprint of the address made with a random value kept in this browser, from which the address cannot be read and against which a guessed address cannot be checked without that value), and, while a save is being written, how far it got, so that it recognises a page sent twice, can undo a save and can finish or remove a save the browser interrupted. For saves from a private (incognito) window this list stays only in memory, until the browser closes. For ten minutes after it reads them, it also keeps the names of your shelves and tags. In Firefox, a renewal token stays in the browser until you sign out; it gives the extension lasting access to Ribband’s folder in your Google Drive, which holds your synced library.

How long. A save waits in your Google Drive until a phone has taken it; the phone deletes it after saving the article. If no phone takes it within 30 days, for example because sync is off on all your phones, the extension deletes it at a later save. If a save fails halfway and the extension cannot confirm that nothing was left, it removes what may be left at a later save; a phone that syncs deletes anything left 30 days after the save in any case. If you remove the extension before that, the save stays in the folder until a phone takes it or you delete the folder’s content (see “Delete your data”). The account’s address, the access and the list stay in the browser until you sign out or remove the extension: “Sign out” forgets the account, the access and the list of its own saves (after that it can no longer undo earlier saves), and removing the extension deletes everything it kept.

Signing out and removing the access. “Sign out” in the extension’s window ends this browser’s access to your Google Drive. It does not take back the access you gave Ribband at Google, because that access belongs to Ribband as a whole: removing it in your Google Account under Linked apps also ends the extension’s access, and your phones sync again only once you allow it there again.

Legal basis. The extension reads and sends a page because you ask it to, to provide the function you use (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). Keeping the account’s address, the access and the list in the browser is strictly necessary for that function (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG).

Google’s user data policy. The use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
