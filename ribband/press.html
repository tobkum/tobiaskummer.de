Ribband, a read-later app for Android

Ribband saves articles and links from any app on an Android phone, tablet or e-reader, and keeps each article as clean text with its pictures to read offline. There is no account and no server of mine behind it: the library stays on the device, and sync, for those who want it, goes through a hidden folder in their own Google Drive.

Browser extension

It needs Ribband on your phone, with sync turned on.

Why it exists

A library that stays on the device

Pocket closed in July 2025, and Omnivore in November 2024. Their lists lived on the services’ own servers, and their users had to take them elsewhere before those servers were cleared. Ribband keeps the library on the device. There is no account to make and no server of mine that could close: sync goes through the user’s own Google Drive, the whole library exports into one file at any time, and the export files of Pocket, Instapaper and Omnivore import, so a list can come along.

When Pocket closed, I wanted a successor for it. The apps I looked at, Instapaper among them, sell their full version as a subscription, and I did not want to pay every month for a reading list. So I wrote my own. Ribband is free to use, and its extra features cost one purchase, once.

How it works

A shared link is saved at once, also offline. The phone then loads the page in a hidden browser view, with its scripts, and two open-source extractors, Mozilla’s Readability and Defuddle, turn it into clean text, with a rule that chooses between them. If no clean text comes out, the reader shows the original page.

Sync needs no server of mine. Each device writes its own numbered change files into a hidden folder of the user’s Google Drive and reads those of the others; when two devices change the same thing, the later change wins, field by field. Article texts travel with the library; pictures do not, each device fetches them from their sites.
